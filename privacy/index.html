Your routines stay on your iPhone.

On your devices.

Your calendar, if you want.

If you switch to dark mode or pick a language, your browser remembers that choice in its local storage on your device; it is never sent anywhere.

Visit counts with Umami

We count visits to this website with Umami, an open-source analytics tool, on an instance we run ourselves at a.not-yet.app. The server is hosted by Hetzner Online GmbH, Industriestraße 25, 91710 Gunzenhausen, Germany, in a data centre in Nuremberg. No analytics company or ad network is involved, and the data is not passed on, not sold and not combined with other data.

For each visit, Umami records the page, the referring page, the browser, the operating system, the device type and the country, which it derives from your IP address. The IP address itself is not stored. We also count clicks on the App Store and TestFlight links, the language menu and the dark mode switch. On NFC tag pages only /t/ is counted, never the tag ID.

Umami sets no cookies, builds no profiles and doesn’t follow you to other websites. We only see totals, which can’t be traced back to you.

The app itself has no analytics and sends nothing to us. The visit counter is for this website only and has no connection to the app.

4. The app: your devices and your iCloud

Not Yet works without an account. Everything you create or change in the app, such as routines, runs, to-dos and their planned times, alarms, bedtime plans, water, coins, rewards, NFC tag links, lock settings and the websites you add to the filter, is stored on your iPhone. The app shares it only with its own widgets, Live Activities and system extensions, your paired Apple Watch and, if you turn on “Add to Calendar”, its own calendar in Apple Calendar (section 6) and, if you turn on the iCloud backup, the private database of your own iCloud (see below).

The app has no server and sends none of this data to us. Apart from the iCloud backup, if you turn it on, it sends it to no one else either. It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools and no third-party code that sends data. It works fully offline; only the iCloud backup needs a connection.

If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, iOS also includes the app’s data in that backup, under Apple’s terms and in your own account. That is one way your routines move to a new iPhone; Not Yet’s own iCloud backup is the other. We have no access to your iCloud. Deleting the app deletes its data from your iPhone, but not your backups.

Not Yet’s iCloud backup (optional)

Not Yet can keep a backup of your data in your own iCloud. It is off by default: only you can turn it on, in the app’s Settings under “iCloud backup” or during setup, and you can turn it off there at any time. While it is on, the app backs up when you leave it, at most once an hour and only if something has changed. When you set up Not Yet on a new or reset iPhone, the app looks for a backup in your iCloud and offers to restore it. Whether it does is up to you.

A backup contains the app’s database, with your routines, to-dos, runs, coins, rewards and kiosk, NFC tag links and settings, plus your alarm, bedtime and lock plans. To tell backups apart, each one also stores the device model, the app version, how many routines, to-dos and coins you have and the number of your last run. Permissions such as Screen Time, alarms, notifications or Apple Health are not part of a backup; you grant them again after a restore.

The backup is stored with Apple’s CloudKit in the private database of your own iCloud account (container iCloud.com.decudo.notyet) and counts against your iCloud storage. Apple stores it encrypted: the copy of the database as an encrypted file, the settings and the details above as encrypted fields. Only the time of each backup and a random ID of the installation stay unencrypted, so the app can sort and tidy up your backups. If you turn on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud, this encryption is end-to-end and only your trusted devices hold the keys; without it, Apple holds them, as for most iCloud data.

We have no access to your private iCloud database, and no server of ours is involved: the backup goes directly from your iPhone to Apple. Apple provides iCloud as its own service under the iCloud terms and Apple’s privacy policy. Where data protection law treats the backup as processing on our part, the legal basis is the app function you chose to use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

The app keeps the last 3 backups of each installation and deletes older ones. It also deletes the backups of an installation that hasn’t made a new one for 180 days. Turning the backup off or deleting the app keeps your existing backups. You can see them in the app under Settings > iCloud backup > Backups. To delete them, open the Settings app on your iPhone and go to [your name] > iCloud > Storage (or Manage Account Storage) > Not Yet.

5. Apple Watch

The Not Yet app on Apple Watch gets your plan for the day directly from your iPhone through Apple’s WatchConnectivity, and sends your taps back the same way. No server is involved, and nothing reaches us.

6. Permissions

Each permission is optional. The app asks only when you use the feature, and you can change your choice at any time in the iOS Settings app.

Notifications and Live Activities

Reminders for routines, planned to-dos, sidequests and water are scheduled locally on your iPhone. They are not sent through a server. The reminder for a planned to-do shows the to-do’s icon, drawn by the app on your iPhone. Live Activities on your Lock Screen are updated on the device.

Alarms

Alarms use Apple’s AlarmKit so they ring even in silent mode. They are scheduled on your iPhone.

Motion and Fitness

If an alarm task asks you to walk a few steps or shake your iPhone, the app reads the step count and motion sensors on the device, only while that task is open. Nothing is stored or sent.

Apple Health (Walking and Water)

Not Yet uses Apple Health only if you turn it on, and asks for permission only then. With Walking, the app reads today’s step count to give you coins for it. With “Sync with Apple Health” under Water, the app writes every glass you log to Health as water and reads today’s water from Health, so glasses from other apps count too. Not Yet reads or writes no other health data. This data stays on your iPhone and in Health, is never sent to us and is never used for advertising. You can withdraw the permission at any time in the Health app.

Calendar

If you allow calendar access, Not Yet reads only the calendars you pick in the app: for birthday to-dos, for ending a sidequest before your next appointment and for showing your appointments in Week. It reads them on your iPhone, never changes them and doesn’t copy them anywhere.

Not Yet writes to Apple Calendar only if you turn on “Add to Calendar” (part of Not Yet Plus). It then creates its own calendar called “Not Yet” and adds your planned to-dos to it: title, time, a short note and a link back to the app, without alerts. Changing a to-do updates its entry; finishing or deleting it removes the entry. Turning the switch off deletes the calendar. If you use iCloud for your calendars, Apple syncs this calendar through iCloud to your devices, like your other calendars; otherwise it stays on your iPhone. Not Yet itself sends nothing anywhere, and nothing reaches us.

Screen Time: Focus lock, Bedtime lock and Adult sites

The locks and the filter for adult websites use Apple’s Screen Time interface (Family Controls). The apps you choose are stored by iOS as opaque tokens on your iPhone. Not Yet can’t see which apps they are, and no usage data leaves your device. The filter for adult websites is Apple’s own filter, applied by iOS on your iPhone; the websites you add yourself are stored only there.

NFC tags

An NFC tag written by Not Yet contains only a link with a random ID, like https://not-yet.app/t/7xk2ab9pqd. Which routine a tag starts is stored only in the app on your iPhone. If a phone without the app scans the tag, it opens that page on this website; the ID says nothing about you, and the visit counter doesn’t record it.

So that such links can open the app, iOS checks a small file from this website through Apple’s servers (Associated Domains). That exchange happens between your iPhone and Apple.

7. Sharing from the app

Run cards are images the app creates on your iPhone. They are only shared when you share them yourself through the iOS share sheet, with the app or person you choose. The same goes for the alarm log, a hidden diagnostic list you can share as text if we ask for it to fix an alarm problem.

8. In-app purchases (Not Yet Plus)

You buy Not Yet Plus in the App Store. Apple handles the purchase, the payment and any subscription renewals. We receive no payment data, no name and no Apple ID. Offer codes and gift codes are redeemed through Apple too.

To know whether Plus is active, the app checks the purchase information that Apple provides through StoreKit on your iPhone. That check happens on the device. The app sends nothing about it to us, and there is no server of ours that could store it. In App Store Connect, Apple shows us only sales figures that don’t identify you.

You manage or cancel a subscription in your Apple Account settings. If you share Plus through Family Sharing, Apple handles that as well. For the payment itself, Apple’s privacy policy applies.

9. App Store and TestFlight

You install Not Yet from Apple’s App Store. If you have turned on “Share With App Developers” in the iOS analytics settings, Apple gives us crash reports and usage figures in App Store Connect. These don’t identify you.

If you test new versions through Apple’s TestFlight and send feedback or screenshots there, or if a test version crashes and you have agreed to share the crash report, Apple passes this on to us in App Store Connect, together with technical data such as device model, iOS version and app version. We use it only to fix problems. Legal basis is our legitimate interest in a working app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Apple’s own processing is covered by Apple’s privacy policy.

10. Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18) and data portability (Art. 20), and the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21). To exercise them, email info@not-yet.app. Since the app keeps your data only on your devices and in your own iCloud, the only personal data we hold are the emails you send us and, if you test the beta, your tester entry in App Store Connect. The visit counts on this website can’t be linked to you.

You can also complain to a data protection supervisory authority.

We update this policy when the app or the website changes. The date at the top shows the current version.
